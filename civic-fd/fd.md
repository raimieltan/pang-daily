For your racing game, I’d make the **Civic FD** feel like a planted, predictable front-wheel-drive car that rewards carrying momentum rather than brute-force drifting.

### Civic FD driving behavior

**Core personality:** stable, slightly nose-heavy, responsive, forgiving, with noticeable FWD understeer when pushed too hard.

- **Low speed:** light and agile. Easy turn-in, easy to place through tight streets.
- **Medium-speed corners:** very confident. The rear stays planted and the car follows steering inputs cleanly.
- **High-speed corners:** increasingly reluctant to rotate. Push too much throttle or steering and the **front tires begin washing outward**.
- **Corner entry:** lifting off the throttle should transfer weight forward and give the rear a subtle rotation. Not a huge slide—just enough for an experienced driver to tighten the line.
- **Mid-corner throttle:** adding throttle stabilizes the rear but starts pulling the car toward understeer.
- **Corner exit:** full throttle too early = front-wheel spin and the car runs wide. Smooth throttle = surprisingly fast exit.
- **Hard braking:** noticeable nose dive, rear becomes slightly lighter, but the car remains controllable.
- **Trail braking:** very useful. Keeping a little brake into the corner helps rotate the chassis.
- **Handbrake:** rear steps out aggressively, but once power comes back on the front wheels pull the car straight again.
- **Oversteer:** usually caused by lift-off, braking, handbrake, bumps, or aggressive weight transfer—not by throttle.

For an **FD2-style sporty/Type R setup**, make everything sharper: faster steering response, stronger front grip, stiffer rear rotation, more aggressive lift-off behavior, much less body roll, and a greater tendency to become twitchy when braking deep into a corner.

The important part is that the Civic shouldn't feel like a drift car. Its fast driving loop should be:

**Brake late → load front tires → rotate on entry → clip apex → progressively add throttle → let FWD pull the car out.**

For your Initial D-style mountain racing, I'd also give it a distinctive behavior where an inexperienced player gets heavy understeer, but a skilled player can make the FD look almost neutral by using **trail braking and lift-off rotation**. That gives the car a high skill ceiling without making it unrealistically oversteery.
