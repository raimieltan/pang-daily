# PANG DAILY — STORY CANON

## 1. Narrative Premise

The player is an ordinary young car enthusiast living in a fictionalized version of Iloilo.

They are not entering the story as a known racer.

They are not rich.

They are not secretly driving a legendary race car.

They simply have access to an old car, not enough money, and enough interest in cars to make questionable choices.

The player's first car is either:

- a family hand-me-down
- an inherited old car
- a cheap Marketplace purchase
- a car bought from a relative or friend

The details may vary, but the emotional starting point is the same:

> **The car is ordinary, old, imperfect, and theirs.**

---

## 2. Story Theme

The central story theme is:

> **Identity through ownership, friendship, struggle, and local reputation.**

The player does not become legendary because they own an expensive car.

They become legendary because people slowly recognize:

- the car
- the driving
- the persistence
- the bad decisions
- the stories attached to the build

---

## 3. Story Tone

The narrative should feel:

- grounded
- warm
- local
- funny
- slightly exaggerated
- conversational
- personal
- nostalgic without being set in the past

The story should not constantly treat racing as life-or-death.

Some of the best moments should be:

- sitting outside a coffee shop
- helping a friend fix something
- arguing over a bad Marketplace purchase
- getting stranded
- driving home after losing
- being teased for a terrible modification
- going on a late-night convoy
- meeting someone who knows someone who knows a mechanic

---

## 4. Cultural Voice

Dialogue should use:

- Taglish
- natural Hiligaynon
- local car-guy slang
- casual Filipino speech
- occasional English when appropriate

Dialogue should not feel like a slang checklist.

Natural example:

> "Pre, ga-init naman temperature mo."

> "Okay pa na."

> "Okay pa? Ara na sa pula."

Another:

> "Boss, good condition gid ni?"

> "Oo boss. Wala issue."

Later:

> "Ngaa ga-kurog ang steering?"

---

## 5. Setting Canon

The game takes place in a fictionalized version of Iloilo City and nearby provincial areas.

The world is inspired by:

- actual coffee shop tambay culture
- Shell gas station meetups
- 7-Eleven-style convenience stops
- local talyers
- vulcanizing shops
- provincial roads
- mountain routes
- roadside homes
- local traffic
- familiar street layouts
- humid tropical nights

The world may remix real inspirations to improve gameplay.

Exact 1:1 recreation is not required.

Recognition is more important than geographic accuracy.

---

## 6. The Player

The player character should not have an overly rigid personality.

They should be defined mostly by:

- choices
- relationships
- driving
- car ownership
- reputation

The story should allow players to roleplay as:

- quiet
- cocky
- funny
- reckless
- practical
- stubborn
- loyal
- opportunistic

The protagonist should not be a silent blank slate, but dialogue choices should allow some flexibility.

---

## 7. Opening Situation

The player begins with:

- little money
- one old car
- limited reputation
- a small social circle
- no major crew affiliation
- no access to serious races

Their first problem is not "become champion."

Their first problem is:

> **Keep the car running.**

The opening should establish:

- maintenance costs
- basic jobs
- local roads
- the talyer
- the coffee shop
- the gas station
- the first friend group
- the first race opportunity

---

## 8. The Coffee Shop

The coffee shop is the primary social hub.

It is inspired by the player's real-world tambay experience.

It should feel:

- casual
- familiar
- warm
- social
- slightly chaotic
- full of parked cars
- full of overlapping conversations

The coffee shop is where players:

- meet rivals
- hear rumors
- get invited to runs
- see new cars
- find sellers
- meet crew members
- receive side stories
- talk after races

The coffee shop is not a mission board.

It is a social place first.

---

## 9. The Talyer

The talyer is the mechanical and narrative anchor.

It should feel like a place the player repeatedly returns to.

It is where:

- damage is diagnosed
- repairs are done
- parts are installed
- bad purchases are discovered
- mechanic relationships deepen
- rumors spread
- story conversations happen

The mechanic NPC should become one of the most important long-term relationships.

---

## 10. The First Mountain Road

The first mountain road is inspired by real roads the player enjoys driving.

It should preserve:

- recognizable corner rhythm
- elevation changes
- roadside landmarks
- mountain atmosphere
- familiar stopover / viewpoint energy

It should be fictionalized enough for gameplay.

The road is not just a race track.

It should feel like a place with:

- traffic
- homes
- road users
- roadside businesses
- dogs and cats
- weather
- local life

Over time, the player should memorize it.

---

## 11. Core NPC Archetypes

The first cast should include at least:

### The Friend Who Enables Bad Decisions

Always supportive of modifications.

Rarely supportive of financial responsibility.

Typical line:

> "Brake pads next month lang. Wheels anay."

### The Mechanic

Practical, experienced, and unimpressed by nonsense.

Can become mentor, friend, or source of rare opportunities.

### The Marketplace Guy

Knows people.

Finds cheap parts.

Sometimes those parts are actually good.

Sometimes they are absolutely not.

### The Local Fast Driver

Does not need an exotic car.

Drives something ordinary extremely well.

Acts as an early proof that road knowledge matters.

### The Porma Guy

Values appearance first.

Can be a source of visual parts, events, and rivalry.

### The Richer Enthusiast

Has access to better cars and better parts.

Should not automatically be a villain.

Represents a different version of the same hobby.

### The Tito

Looks harmless.

Drives something old and understated.

Is suspiciously fast.

May become a late mentor or hidden benchmark.

---

## 12. Crews

Crews represent different local car subcultures.

Potential crews:

- mountain/touge crew
- drag crew
- porma/stance crew
- old-school club
- gas-station tambay crowd
- wealthy enthusiast group
- street point-to-point crowd

Crews should:

- have identifiable personalities
- have different vehicle tastes
- prefer different race styles
- offer different relationships
- overlap socially

They are not gang factions.

People can still know each other across crews.

---

## 13. Chapter 1 — "Pang Daily"

### Goal

Introduce the player to the world, finances, local scene, and first meaningful race.

### Opening

The player receives or purchases their old starter car.

The car is not in perfect condition.

The player has enough money for basic needs, but not enough to freely modify it.

### Early Beats

1. Learn basic driving.
2. Visit the talyer.
3. Learn that something needs repair.
4. Complete a small job to earn money.
5. Meet friends at the coffee shop.
6. See the local scene.
7. Hear about a low-stakes race.
8. Decide whether to spend on maintenance or a cheap upgrade.
9. Enter the first race.
10. Discover that the car is either slower, less reliable, or both.

### First Race

The first race should not be a dramatic final-boss event.

It should feel like:

> "Sige, try lang."

The first race may be:

- a short point-to-point
- a simple drag
- a small mountain climb

The player does not need to win.

Losing should still progress the story.

### First Setback

A mechanical or financial setback should occur shortly after.

Examples:

- worn tire
- overheating
- brake issue
- cheap part failure
- fine
- repair bill

This teaches the player:

> Racing costs money.

### Chapter End

The player begins to become recognized by name or by car.

Not famous.

Just known enough that someone says:

> "Ikaw gali na ang ga-drive sang puti nga sedan?"

That is the first reputation milestone.

---

## 14. Mid-Game Narrative

As reputation grows:

- better races become available
- stronger drivers appear
- crew invitations begin
- rare parts become accessible
- rivalries become more personal
- player friendships deepen
- police pressure becomes more relevant
- the player's car becomes visually recognizable
- ownership of a second car becomes possible

The story should avoid a single simplistic villain.

Conflict should come from:

- rivalry
- ego
- money
- loyalty
- bad decisions
- accidents
- crew tension
- differing philosophies

---

## 15. Endgame Narrative

The endgame is not "win the national championship."

The player becomes a local legend.

That can mean:

- their car is recognized
- crews know them
- new players ask about them
- old rivals respect them
- mechanics trust them
- they own multiple cars
- they have stories attached to each build
- they are invited to high-level races
- they have become part of the local scene's mythology

A possible final emotional beat:

A newcomer asks an NPC:

> "Sin-o na?"

The NPC answers:

> "Ah. Siya na."

No long explanation needed.

---

## 16. Relationship Philosophy

NPC relationships should persist.

The player can:

- befriend rivals
- lose friends
- gain mentors
- owe favors
- earn discounts
- unlock secret opportunities
- make enemies
- repair relationships

Relationships should not be reduced to a simple romance-style meter.

They should be based on:

- trust
- history
- shared events
- favors
- race outcomes
- decisions
- reliability

---

## 17. Story Rules

### Rule 1

The player is never secretly rich.

### Rule 2

Ordinary cars are narratively important.

### Rule 3

Rare cars remain rare.

### Rule 4

Local reputation matters more than global fame.

### Rule 5

Friends and mechanics matter as much as rivals.

### Rule 6

Humor should come from believable situations.

### Rule 7

The player should fail sometimes without the story collapsing.

### Rule 8

Cars should accumulate history.

### Rule 9

Locations should feel lived in.

### Rule 10

The game should always feel like Iloilo, not generic Japan.
