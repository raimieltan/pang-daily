# PANG DAILY — GAME VISION

## Working Title

**Pang Daily**

A Filipino car-life RPG about being broke, keeping old cars alive, making questionable financial decisions, racing local roads, and slowly becoming a legend in Iloilo's underground car scene.

---

## 1. High Concept

Pang Daily is a modern-day, Iloilo-inspired car culture RPG centered on one core fantasy:

> **Surviving as a broke car guy.**

The player is not a wealthy collector or professional racer. They are an ordinary enthusiast with limited money, an aging car, friends who encourage bad financial decisions, and a local scene built around talyers, gas stations, coffee shops, convenience stores, roadside stops, and mountain roads.

Racing matters, but it is not the entire game.

The game is about:

- keeping an old car running
- deciding whether to repair something or buy another mod
- hunting for cheap secondhand parts
- earning money through jobs, deliveries, racing, reselling parts, and eventually car flipping
- building relationships with people in the local car scene
- learning roads deeply
- becoming known for doing too much with too little

---

## 2. Player Fantasy

The player should feel like:

- a broke enthusiast trying to keep a project alive
- someone whose car is slowly becoming recognizable
- someone who knows where to find cheap parts
- someone who has friends, rivals, mechanics, and contacts all over the scene
- someone who learns a road so well that a weak car can still be dangerous
- someone who can become a local legend without ever becoming rich

The ideal emotional progression is:

```text
"Why am I driving this thing?"
        ↓
"Okay, it's getting better."
        ↓
"Wait, this car is actually quick."
        ↓
"People are starting to recognize it."
        ↓
"That's MY car."
        ↓
"Everyone knows that car."
```

---

## 3. Core Design Pillars

### 3.1 Broke Car Guy Survival

Money should always matter.

The player constantly chooses between:

- fuel
- repairs
- registration
- fines
- tires
- food / small life expenses
- race entry
- visual mods
- performance mods
- saving for another car

The player should frequently be able to afford **some things, but not everything**.

Bad financial decisions are part of the fun.

---

### 3.2 Local Recognition

The world should feel specifically Iloilo-inspired, not generically Filipino.

The desired reaction from local players is:

> "Oh shit, that's familiar."

Familiarity should come from:

- recognizable road rhythms
- mountain roads inspired by real drives
- coffee shop tambay spots
- Shell-style gas station meets
- 7-Eleven-style convenience stops
- talyers
- vulcanizing shops
- roadside eateries
- provincial roadside scenery
- traffic behavior
- tricycles
- motorcycles
- cats and dogs
- Taglish and Hiligaynon dialogue
- local humor
- mundane details people actually recognize

The world may be fictionalized for gameplay, but it should preserve real local memory.

---

### 3.3 Cheap Cars Matter

The game should celebrate ordinary old cars.

Starter archetypes are inspired by common 1990s–2000s cars such as:

- Mitsubishi Lancer
- Honda Civic
- Toyota Corolla
- Nissan Sentra

Cars should be fictionalized, but grounded in what is actually common or plausible in Iloilo.

Rare performance cars should feel rare.

A clean, unusual, or genuinely fast car should create excitement.

---

### 3.4 FWD Is Legitimate

Front-wheel drive should never feel like a temporary beginner category.

High-skill FWD driving should matter through:

- momentum conservation
- late braking
- trail braking
- managing understeer
- lift-off rotation
- throttle discipline
- road knowledge

Grip driving should be fully viable.

---

### 3.5 Social Car Culture

The player is part of a scene, not a menu.

NPCs can become:

- friends
- mechanics
- sellers
- crew members
- enemies
- mentors
- buyers
- rivals

A rival beaten early in the story should still exist later.

Relationships should unlock:

- races
- rare parts
- better prices
- secret roads
- mechanic access
- new crews
- higher-stakes events
- story beats

---

### 3.6 Cars Become Personal

Cars should gradually feel owned, not merely unlocked.

Players should be able to:

- change wheels
- alter ride height
- change exterior parts
- build ugly cars
- build tasteful cars
- build monstrosities
- maintain multiple cars if they can afford them

The game should allow terrible taste.

The emotional goal is attachment, not optimization.

---

## 4. Tone

The tone is:

- grounded
- warm
- nostalgic
- slightly exaggerated
- funny
- proudly local
- never self-serious for too long

Humor should feel like real car-guy humor.

Example tone:

> "Pre, brake pads mo daw papel na."

> "Next sweldo ko na na."

> "Amo man na hambal mo last month."

Then the friend notices the new wheels.

> "...Pila na?"

> "Don't ask."

The game should feel affectionate toward local car culture, not mocking.

---

## 5. Setting

### Time

Modern day.

### Place

A fictionalized but clearly Iloilo-inspired city and provincial road network.

The game should include:

- a dense local hub
- city-edge roads
- provincial connectors
- one major mountain route for the first release
- recognizable roadside businesses and landmarks
- social spaces for car meets

### World Philosophy

Do not recreate all of Iloilo.

Build small, memorable slices.

The first playable world should feel like:

```text
PLAYER HOME
   │
   ├── Neighborhood
   ├── Talyer
   ├── Vulcanizing Shop
   ├── Car Wash
   ├── Convenience Store
   ├── Gas Station
   ├── Coffee Shop Tambay
   └── Main Road
         │
         └── Mountain Route
```

---

## 6. Core Gameplay Loop

```text
EARN MONEY
    ↓
MAINTAIN / REPAIR CAR
    ↓
BUY QUESTIONABLE PARTS
    ↓
INSTALL / CUSTOMIZE
    ↓
DRIVE / TEST
    ↓
TAMBAY / BUILD RELATIONSHIPS
    ↓
RACE / TAKE JOBS
    ↓
GAIN MONEY + REPUTATION
    ↓
BREAK SOMETHING
    ↓
REPEAT
```

---

## 7. Money Sources

The player can earn money through:

- deliveries
- part-time work
- talyer jobs
- courier work
- car wash work
- buying and reselling parts
- racing
- race bets
- car flipping later in progression

Racing should not be the only income source.

The player should sometimes use a modified car for ordinary work.

---

## 8. Racing Modes

Initial focus:

### Drag

Accessible and power-focused.

Good for early progression and testing basic upgrades.

### Touge

Skill-focused.

Can support:

- uphill
- downhill
- time attack
- 1v1 battles

### Street Point-to-Point

A signature mode.

Features:

- traffic
- route knowledge
- live road hazards
- motorcycles
- tricycles
- cats
- dogs
- unpredictable conditions

This mode should feel specifically Filipino.

---

## 9. Driving Feel

Target handling reference:

**Initial D Arcade Stage-like responsiveness**

The game should be:

- arcade-first
- immediately readable
- easy to pick up
- deeper than it first appears

The player should feel:

- FWD understeer
- lift-off rotation
- AWD traction
- RWD oversteer where applicable
- grip differences from tires
- braking differences
- weight differences
- road surface differences
- rain effects

The goal is not full simulation.

The goal is:

> **believable arcade handling with meaningful build differences**

---

## 10. Traffic and Road Life

Traffic should make roads feel alive.

Traffic types may include:

- tricycles
- motorcycles
- jeepney-like public transport
- sedans
- vans
- delivery trucks
- pedestrians
- cats
- dogs

Traffic should create readable situations rather than random unavoidable punishment.

---

## 11. Damage and Consequences

Bad decisions should hurt.

The player may:

- overheat the engine
- destroy a transmission
- wear out a clutch
- damage suspension
- ruin tires
- crash badly
- waste money on bad parts
- buy broken parts
- receive fines
- face impound or registration consequences

The player should never be fully protected from their own bad decisions.

---

## 12. Reputation

Reputation is one of the main progression systems.

Example ladder:

```text
Unknown
Regular
Known
Respected
Feared
Local Legend
```

Reputation unlocks:

- better races
- higher stakes
- rarer parts
- secret roads
- stronger opponents
- better sellers
- mechanics
- crews
- car meets
- story progression

---

## 13. Crews

The world should include distinct local subcultures.

Potential crew types:

- gas-station tambay crew
- drag guys
- mountain/touge drivers
- stance/porma crew
- old-school club
- richer enthusiast crowd
- highway / point-to-point crowd

Crews are social identities, not just enemy factions.

---

## 14. Police and Legal Consequences

Illegal activity can attract consequences.

Potential consequences:

- fines
- impound
- registration issues
- lost race entry
- reputation changes
- vehicle damage from fleeing
- temporary access restrictions

Police should not behave like a GTA wanted-level system.

Sometimes the smartest decision should be to stop.

---

## 15. Starter Cars

The player chooses from four fictionalized starter archetypes inspired by:

- Lancer
- Civic
- Corolla
- Sentra

Each should have:

- different condition
- different weight
- different handling
- different reliability
- different parts availability
- different reputation in the scene

Starter origins may include:

- hand-me-down
- inherited car
- cheap Marketplace buy
- bought from a relative or friend

The mechanical effect of origin can be small.

---

## 16. Character and Walking Gameplay

The player should be able to leave the car.

Walking is used for:

- talyer interaction
- coffee shop scenes
- car meets
- junkyards
- shops
- inspecting cars
- talking to NPCs
- walking around parking areas
- social storytelling

Walking is not intended to become a combat or open-world action system.

---

## 17. Multiplayer

Long-term multiplayer goal:

**1v1 versus racing**

Initial multiplayer should focus on:

- direct challenges
- synced race state
- position
- rotation
- velocity
- checkpoints
- countdown
- finish time

Multiplayer is not part of the first core MVP.

Single-player must work first.

---

## 18. Endgame Fantasy

The player does not need to become the richest person in the city.

The endgame fantasy is:

- becoming a local legend
- owning a recognizable build
- having a small collection
- being known by crews
- having access to rare events and roads
- becoming part of the culture itself

The player may still be broke.

That is part of the joke.

---

## 19. MVP Scope

The first serious playable target should contain:

- 1 starter sedan
- 1 connected local hub
- 1 talyer
- 1 coffee shop social hub
- 1 gas station
- 1 convenience store
- 1 mountain road
- arcade vehicle handling
- enter/exit vehicle
- 3–5 NPCs
- 1 rival
- 1 race
- 1 job type
- 1 repair loop
- 1 Marketplace system
- 10–15 parts
- visible wheel / ride height customization
- basic money
- basic reputation
- basic damage

If this loop is fun, expand from there.

---

## 20. What Pang Daily Is Not

Pang Daily is not:

- a realistic motorsport simulator
- a giant open-world racer
- a Japanese street-racing clone
- a cyberpunk racing game
- a supercar collection game
- a drift-only game
- an MMO
- a vehicle physics research project
- a photorealistic AAA game

It is a **local car-life RPG with arcade driving**.

---

## 21. Success Criteria

The game succeeds if players say things like:

- "This feels like Iloilo."
- "That road looks familiar."
- "That NPC is literally my friend."
- "I spent my repair money on wheels."
- "I can't believe this shitbox is actually fast."
- "I know this road by memory now."
- "That car is ugly, but I love it."
- "Amo gid ni."
